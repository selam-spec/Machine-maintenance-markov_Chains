Machine-maintenance-markov_Chains
This project models a production machine as a four-state Markov chain (Working, Degraded, Broken and Under Repair) to estimate how much of the time it is actually available to produce, and to compare two maintenance strategies: servicing machines as soon as they start to wear, or waiting until they break. I built it to apply what I learned in my Random Processes module to a real operations decision, and to develop my Python skills from the ground up, by writing the validity checks, simulation, stationary-distribution solver and convergence plots myself.
Under my assumptions, preventive servicing raises long-run availability from 81.5% to 88.1%, as most episodes of wear are caught and fixed with a quick service before they turn into breakdowns that need longer repairs. The notebook also sets out the model's limitations, including the memoryless assumption and the use of assumed rather than estimated probabilities, along with next steps such as comparing the policies on cost.
